Every email must be sent from a domain your team has **verified** — the API
rejects any other `from` address with `422`. Domains are added and verified in
the dashboard.

## Verification

Adding a domain registers it with SES and shows the DNS records to add:

- **DKIM** — a single `millionsend._domainkey` TXT record. MillionSend
  generates an RSA-2048 keypair per domain and hands the private key to SES
  (BYODKIM), so verification is one TXT record instead of three CNAMEs. The
  private key is never stored.
- **MAIL FROM** — MX and SPF (TXT) records for the bounce subdomain.

On the cloud a team adds up to 10 domains an hour, or 100 on Scale. The count
restarts on every full hour (UTC); past it the API answers
`429 rate_limit_exceeded` with `retry-after`, and the dashboard says how many
minutes are left. A domain that isn't verified within 72 hours of being added
is deleted, so add each one when you're ready to publish its records.

Two verification signals are shown side by side:

- **SES status** — what SES reports. SES caches verification and can lag
  after you change records.
- **Live DNS check** — MillionSend resolves each record itself and reports
  Found / Missing / Mismatch immediately.

The API (`GET /domains/{id}`, `POST /domains/{id}/verify`, and the `get_domain`
/ `verify_domain` MCP tools) reports the same picture per record in `records[]`.
`status` uses the Resend vocabulary: for DKIM and MAIL FROM it combines the live
check with SES — found in DNS but not yet confirmed by SES reads `pending`, a
different published value reads `failed`, no record reads `not_started`. Only
those rows gate sending. The DMARC row follows RFC 7489 discovery: it reads
`verified` when a policy covers the domain, including a parent domain's record
for a subdomain sender — then `inherited_from` names the `_dmarc` record that
answered and `policy` carries its `p=` value — and `not_started` when none does.
Every record also carries `live` (`found`, `missing`, `mismatch` or `unknown`,
what public DNS answers right now) and, when a row is not verified, a one-line
`detail` saying why.

## Regions

A deployment provisions identities in the SES regions it serves — its
`AWS_REGIONS`, or the one region in `AWS_REGION`; MillionSend Cloud serves
`sa-east-1` (São Paulo) today. The dashboard's add-domain form lists the
served regions, holds back one still in the SES sandbox while another has
production access, and defaults to the first production region. The API's
optional `region` field (the `create_domain` MCP tool included) accepts any
served region — the values its schema lists — and defaults to the first;
any other value is rejected with `422` naming the served regions.
Configuration sets, event topics and SES tenants are regional, so a domain
anywhere else would hand out DNS records but never send or report events.

A domain has exactly one region. To move it, delete it and add it again in
the other region — its DNS records change, since SES identities are per
region. On MillionSend Cloud a domain name another team holds is taken in
every region.

## New domain limit

On MillionSend Cloud a domain registered recently sends gradually while it
builds a reputation with mailbox providers. The age is the registered
domain's, so `mail.acme.com` counts as `acme.com`, and a name from a free
subdomain service, such as `shop.eu.org`, counts as its own domain. It is
read from public data when the domain is added, and the cap counts per UTC
day:

| Domain age | Emails per day |
| --- | --- |
| Under 24 hours | 100 |
| 1 to 7 days | 300 |
| 7 to 30 days | 2,000 |
| 30 days or older | your plan's limit only |

A domain that sends cleanly (few bounces, no spam complaints) moves up a step
sooner. The cap applies on top of your plan, and a higher plan doesn't
change it.

Your team's new domains share one daily volume, the highest of their caps,
and each still stays within its own: adding another new domain doesn't add
volume. Domains 30 days or older aren't part of it. When more than one of
your domains is under this limit, the domain page says the volume is shared.

This limit is automatic and separate from the opt-in [warm-up](#warm-up)
below. When both apply, a broadcast goes out within the lower of the two.

### What you see

- **Emails:** each email the limit holds reads **Waiting · daily limit**,
  and **When** shows the time it goes out after, in your time zone. A banner
  above the list says how many emails wait for which domain, and that they
  go out on their own after 00:00 UTC.
- **An email's page** says it waits for the domain's daily limit, how many
  of today's emails went out, how many waiting emails are ahead of it, and
  when it should go out.
- **Domains:** a domain under the limit shows its daily volume and how many
  emails wait. Its tooltip has the schedule.
- **The domain's page** has a **New domain limit** section with what the
  domain sent today, what waits, how the limit grows and when it ends.
- **Emails to your team's owners:** **Your domain is verified** says the
  domain starts with a daily limit and when it ends. **Your new domain
  reached today's limit** says how many emails wait, at most once a day per
  domain. Turn it off under **Domains** in **Settings → Notifications**.

### Mail that waits

Mail over the day's cap is never refused. It is accepted, waits, and goes
out on its own after 00:00 UTC, oldest first, with transactional email ahead
of broadcasts, so there's no need to resend it. Nothing that waits is lost
while email content is kept: 30 days on Cloud, the instance's email retention
when self-hosted.

Through the API, a send the limit holds answers with a `warning`:

```json
{
  "id": "4f1c9a2e-7b3d-4e8a-9c61-2d5f8e0b7a14",
  "warning": {
    "code": "new_domain_limit",
    "message": "nova.example sent its 300 emails for today. This email goes out after 00:00 UTC.",
    "sends_after": "2026-10-21T00:00:00.000Z"
  }
}
```

Each item of `POST /emails/batch` the limit holds carries the same
`warning`. `GET /emails/{id}` and `GET /emails` keep `last_event: "queued"`,
as Resend clients expect, and add `queued_reason: "new_domain_limit"` with
`sends_after`, the earliest the email goes out. Only when a lot of mail is
already waiting does a send answer `429 daily_quota_exceeded` (SMTP `452`),
with a message naming the limit and a `Retry-After` header that counts to
00:00 UTC.

### Ask for a higher limit

When the domain takes over mail that already has a sending history, such as
a product moving from another email provider, choose **Ask for a higher
limit** on the domain's page. Say what it sends, about how many emails a day,
and where it goes out today. Owners and admins can ask, with one open request
per domain. Mail keeps going out at the current pace meanwhile, and the team's
owners and whoever asked get an email with the answer. You can also reply to
either email above.

## Warm-up

Mailbox providers trust a new sender a little more each day it behaves.
Warm-up (beta) builds on that: while it is on for a domain, the domain's
broadcasts go out under a daily volume that starts small and grows each day
delivery stays healthy, most active contacts first. It is off by default and
applies to one domain at a time. Your transactional email is never held.

Turn it on in the dashboard: on the domain's **Warm-up** tab, when you add
the domain (answer "No, it's new to sending" to "Has this domain sent email
before?"), or in the **Send broadcast** dialog, which offers it when a
broadcast is far bigger than anything the domain has sent in a day.

### When to use it

Turn it on for a new domain or subdomain before its first big send, or
before a send far bigger than anything the domain has sent in a day.

Leave it off for a domain that already sends this volume through another
provider. It keeps the reputation it has there. A domain that sends less
elsewhere can still warm up from what it sends every day: raise the starting
volume to match.

### How it works

- Day 1 sends the starting volume: 1,500, or twice the domain's busiest day
  of the last 30 days when that is more. You can set it from 100 to
  1,000,000.
- Each healthy day grows the volume 1.5×, up to the daily target, the most
  you plan to send in a day. The target starts at your number of subscribed
  contacts, at least 10,000, and you can set it from 1,000 to 10,000,000.
  The warm-up ends after a healthy day at the target.
- A day's volume goes out a 48th at a time, every 15 minutes, so it takes at
  least 12 hours. Days are UTC days, and each one starts only after the day
  before it is checked.
- Mail over the day's volume waits for the next day, in the same order, and
  shows as **Waiting**, for as long as email content is kept (see below).
- Only broadcasts wait. Email sent with `POST /emails`, in a batch or
  through SMTP is never held.
- A day that sends less than half its volume doesn't grow it. Days without a
  send don't count, and after 30 of them in a row the volume starts over.

### Who goes first

Each day's volume goes to the contacts most likely to open it, ranked by the
newest of these signals. You choose which ones count on the **Warm-up** tab.

- Their last open or click. A machine's prefetch never counts.
- The last transactional email they got from your team in the last 30 days.
- A contact property you pick, read as a date, such as a last login. It
  takes an ISO 8601 date or date-time, or Unix time in seconds or
  milliseconds between the years 2000 and 2100, so a count never reads as a
  date. The list shows your [date properties](/concepts/contacts#property-types)
  first, then other properties where at least half the values are dates,
  read from your 20,000 most recently updated contacts.

A contact whose newest signal is 90 days old or more is inactive. By default
the warm-up skips inactive contacts: they don't get the broadcast, and the
broadcast page shows how many were skipped. Choose **Send them last** to put
them at the end of the line instead. A contact with no signal at all is not
inactive: it goes after the active ones.

### The daily check

Early each UTC day, the day before is checked on five signals, read from the
domain's broadcast mail of that day. Gmail is measured apart from other
inboxes.

| Signal | What it reads | Line | Severe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery delays | Gmail mail that Gmail delayed | under 5% | 20% or more |
| Opens, Gmail vs. others | Gmail's open rate as a share of other inboxes' | at least half | under a quarter |
| Hard bounces | Addresses that bounced for good | under 2% | 5% or more |
| Full or inactive mailboxes | Mailboxes that answered full or inactive | under 2% | 5% or more |
| Spam complaints | Complaints over mail to providers that report them | under 0.05% | 0.1% or more |

A signal past its line is missed. Gmail never tells senders about spam
reports, so complaints come from the other providers, and your Gmail spam
rate is in [Google Postmaster Tools](https://postmaster.google.com). Opens
are read only while [open tracking](#per-domain-configuration) is on, and a
machine's prefetch never counts as one. A signal with too little mail behind
it (fewer than 100 emails, or 200 for complaints) reads **Not enough mail**
and never counts against the day. It takes two complaints to miss the
complaint line and three to make it severe.

After the check:

- Every signal within its line: the volume grows 1.5×.
- One missed signal: the volume holds.
- Two or more missed: the volume drops by 30%.
- A severe signal: the warm-up pauses and the team's owners get an email.

### Pause, resume or turn off

- **Pause warm-up** stops the domain's broadcasts. Their mail waits in order.
  A severe signal pauses the warm-up the same way.
- **Resume** starts again from today at half the volume it had.
- **Turn off** ends the warm-up, and the mail that was waiting goes out at
  normal speed. A big day from a domain with little history is likely to land
  in spam, so the dashboard asks first.
- Once the warm-up is done, the domain's broadcasts go out at normal speed.
  **Warm up again** starts over from the starting volume.

Waiting mail keeps its place for as long as email content is kept, counted
from when the broadcast was sent: 30 days on Cloud, the instance's email
retention when self-hosted. Mail still waiting after that is not sent.

The team's owners get an email when a severe signal pauses the warm-up, with
the signals that missed and how many emails are waiting, and another when the
warm-up is done. Owners choose these under **Deliverability** in
**Settings → Notifications**.

### Should I switch to a new subdomain instead?

No. Mailbox providers judge reputation by the organizational domain
(`example.com` for `news.example.com`) and by the people you send to, so a
new subdomain doesn't start clean. Rotating domains looks like snowshoeing,
the spammer trick of spreading mail over many domains, and providers watch
for it. Warm up the domain you have.

## Per-domain configuration

Once verified, a domain's **Configuration** tab controls:

- **Click tracking** — off by default. When on, links are rewritten to redirect
  through your own tracking subdomain to record `email.clicked` events, then send
  the recipient on to the original URL. When off, your links ship untouched.
- **Open tracking** — off by default. When on, a 1×1 pixel served from that same
  tracking subdomain records `email.opened` events. Tracking is app-layer and
  runs on your own domain — never SES's link rewriting. Mail from a people
  inbox is never tracked, as from any mail client.
- **TLS mode** — `opportunistic` (default) or `enforced`, applied via the
  domain's SES configuration set.

Through the API and MCP, `update_domain` takes the same tracking settings, and
`create_domain` accepts them as optional extras so a tracked domain can be stood
up in one call (a Resend-shaped call that omits them is unchanged).
Tracking is served from the domain's own tracking subdomain: pass
`tracking_subdomain` (a label such as `links`) and the response's `records[]`
gains a Tracking CNAME whose status turns `verified` once it resolves. On
MillionSend Cloud, turning either kind on without a subdomain is refused with a
422. Until the CNAME resolves, links are not routed through it — Cloud ships
them clean, self-host falls back to the app host — and the domain shows as
**Partial** in the dashboard: verified for sending, tracking not yet live. On
Cloudflare the tracking record must stay **DNS only** (grey cloud): a proxied
CNAME answers with Cloudflare's addresses instead of the target, which the
records table reports as a mismatch, and TLS for the tracking host is served
by MillionSend. A tracking host redirects only the links of the team whose
domain holds it, and a domain holds its tracking host once verified, even if it
later loses verification. On MillionSend Cloud a host no domain holds redirects
nothing: after you change the tracking subdomain or delete the domain or the
team, links already sent through the old host stop redirecting, and they resume
only if the team adds the domain back with the same tracking subdomain and it
verifies. A self-hosted instance redirects every team's links from a host no
domain holds, so remove the tracking CNAME when you give a host up.

## Open-rate accuracy

Open tracking injects a 1×1 transparent pixel with a unique reference into the
HTML body; a person loading that image records an `email.opened` event. It is
a directional signal, not an exact count.

Fetches a machine plausibly made are recorded as **prefetched**, not opened:
Apple Mail Privacy Protection downloads every image in the background whether
or not the message is read, Gmail pre-fetches while the inbox is already open,
and security scanners fetch the pixel within seconds of delivery. A prefetch
shows on the email's timeline and as its own line under the open rate, but it
never moves the status, never counts in the open rate, and never fires
`email.opened` (endpoints can opt in to `email.prefetched`). The timing rule's
window is `OPEN_PREFETCH_WINDOW_SECONDS` for self-hosters (default `10`; `0`
keeps only the user-agent rules). Links go through the same rules, plus two
of their own: a desktop Chrome that reports a build number no browser has sent
since Chrome's user-agent reduction, and two links of a message hit within a
quarter of a second, are a machine's whatever they call themselves. A click recorded before
the rest of its burst arrived is taken back — row, inferred open, counters,
status and any delivery not yet posted.

A click is also an open. A person cannot click a link in a message they never
rendered, so a click on an email with no open yet records the open as well,
stamped just before the click and marked `reason: "click"`. For a recipient
whose images come through Apple Mail's cache this is the only open that can
ever be recorded, so Apple-heavy audiences read lower on opens than they would
elsewhere until they click.

Opens are **under-counted** when the recipient's client blocks images, when the
email has no HTML part (a plain-text send carries no pixel), or when Gmail clips
a message over ~102 KB and the recipient never expands it.

Clicks are the more reliable engagement signal. For purely transactional mail —
receipts, password resets — consider leaving open tracking off: the pixel adds a
tracking-shaped element some filters weigh against inbox placement, for a metric
you can't fully trust anyway.

## Receiving

A verified domain can also [receive mail](/concepts/receiving) (beta). Every
domain response carries Resend's `capabilities` object: `sending` is
`enabled` (asking to disable it is a `422`; only the
[shared subdomain](#shared-subdomain) can read `disabled`), and `receiving`
is `enabled` or `disabled`. Switch receiving with **Enable receiving** on
the domain page, or send `{ "capabilities": { "receiving": "enabled" } }`
(or `"disabled"`) to `PATCH /domains/{id}`. Only a verified domain can turn
it on, which is why
`POST /domains` refuses `"receiving": "enabled"` with a `422`: add the domain,
verify it, then switch receiving on. A domain name receives in one team only:
a name another team already receives on answers `409`. On an instance that
doesn't receive mail at all, every domain reads `disabled` and turning it on
is refused with `422`.

While receiving is on, `records[]` includes a `Receiving` row: an MX record
at the domain's own name pointing at `mx.millionsend-dns.com` (self-hosted,
the instance's `RECEIVING_MX_HOST`), priority `10`.

```json
{
  "record": "Receiving",
  "name": "inbound.example.com",
  "type": "MX",
  "ttl": "Auto",
  "status": "verified",
  "value": "mx.millionsend-dns.com",
  "priority": 10,
  "live": "found"
}
```

The row is checked live like the others: `verified` once that host is among
the name's MX records with the lowest priority number, `failed` while another
MX record on the name has a lower number (`detail` lists what is published),
and `not_started` with no MX record at all. A lower-numbered MX record takes
the mail instead, which is why the MX belongs on a subdomain such as
`inbound.example.com` when the domain already receives mail elsewhere. The
row never gates sending.

`receiving_catch_all` says whether the domain takes mail at every address
(`true`) or only at its [inboxes'](/concepts/inboxes) addresses (`false`,
the default; other addresses are refused). Owners and admins switch it with
**Catch-all** on the domain page or `PATCH /domains/{id}` with
`{ "receiving_catch_all": true }`; on MillionSend Cloud it needs a paid plan.
See [Which addresses receive](/concepts/receiving#which-addresses-receive).
Deleting a domain deletes its inboxes, with their threads and drafts, and
revokes their API keys.

## Shared subdomain

On an instance with a [shared inbox domain](/concepts/inboxes#shared-domain),
`GET /domains` ends with one more row: your team's subdomain of it, such as
`acme.agents.example`, where your shared inboxes get their addresses; a
team whose subdomain still waits for its name review has none yet. Every
row carries `shared`, and this is the one with `shared: true`. It reads
`verified`, in the instance's default region, with receiving on, catch-all
and tracking off, and two fields of its own:

- `sending_mode`: what addresses on it may send now: `open` (start
  conversations), `reply_only` (reply inside their threads) or
  `receive_only` (nothing goes out, and `capabilities.sending` reads
  `disabled`). An inbox whose address or `from_name` looks like a
  well-known brand only replies, even when the mode is `open` (see
  [Shared domain](/concepts/inboxes#shared-domain)).
- `sending_mode_reason`: why it is below `open`: `plan` (the team's plan)
  or `instance` (the instance's settings); `null` when open.

The row is read-only: `GET /domains/{id}` answers it with `records: []`,
since there is nothing to set up, and `PATCH`, `POST /domains/{id}/verify`
and `DELETE` answer `403 forbidden`. It doesn't count toward your plan's
domain limit, and its id stays the same when a rename changes its name.
Though verified, it sends only from your inboxes' own addresses, and
`POST /api-keys` refuses its id as `domain_id` with `422 validation_error`.
To create an inbox on it, send its id as `domain_id` to `POST /inboxes`.
The dashboard's **Domains** page shows it first, marked **Shared**.

## API keys and domains

An API key can be scoped to a single domain; such a key can only send from
that domain (other domains return `403 restricted_api_key`), and reads only
the mail [received](/concepts/receiving) at it. Keys it creates
through the API are scoped to the same domain: an omitted `domain_id` inherits
it, and any other domain returns `403 restricted_api_key`.

A key can be scoped to one [inbox](/concepts/inboxes#inbox-scoped-api-keys)
instead, which narrows it further: that inbox's mail, and sends from its
address only.
